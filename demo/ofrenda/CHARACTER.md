# Eufemia "Femia" Archuleta, and the ofrenda kept for her

Fiction, every word: Femia, Marisol, Joaquín, Rosa and the rest are no one.
The places are real (Truchas, Española, the Santuario de Chimayó) and so are
the customs; the people are made up.

**Femia** (1938–2022) was born in Truchas, up on the High Road between Santa
Fe and Taos, and lived most of her life in Española. She cooked in an
elementary-school cafeteria for thirty-one years. Her biscochitos won the
church bake sale every December her sister **Rosa** didn't enter, and most
of the ones she did. At seventy-one she learned the computer: her grandson
**Joaquín**, a programmer in Denver, put Ubuntu on an old Dell in 2010 and
taught her the terminal, because "Grandma, it's just typing." She kept her
recipes there, wrote to her cousin **Lupe** in Alamosa, and once, in October
2010, asked him to show her how to make the computer do something by itself.

**Marisol** (34), her granddaughter, lives in Femia's house in Española now.
She keeps the ofrenda up year round in the sala: Femia's photo, a veladora
of the Virgen de Guadalupe, her rosary, a ristra, a little bag of tierra
bendita from Chimayó, marigolds from the yard when there are marigolds, a
plate of biscochitos, and a cup of coffee with canela. The old Dell is on
the table beside it, still plugged in, because nobody could bring
themselves to turn it off.

## How her life writes itself down

| where | what |
|---|---|
| `computadora-de-femia/bash_history` | Femia's terminal, 2010–2021, each command stamped by bash |
| `computadora-de-femia/recetas/` | her recipes as she typed them: biscochitos, chile colorado, posole, natillas |
| `computadora-de-femia/notas/` | what she wrote down while learning the computer |
| `computadora-de-femia/cartas/` | letters to Lupe |
| `computadora-de-femia/vela.sh` | a four-line script |
| `marisol/` | Marisol's diary since the funeral, and the list of what's on the ofrenda |

## What Marisol wants from a tool

> "Tía Rosa says there was something in Grandma's biscochitos that isn't on
> the card. Grandma just winked. I don't want a computer making up what she
> would have said. I want to know what she actually wrote down — and if she
> didn't, I want it to say so."
