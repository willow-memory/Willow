# Homestead's rungs and gates, beside willow's seal and record

Written 2026-11-11 from the same box, and from willow's own kernel and
constitution. A reading of two designs at one moment, made to see where
a willow that held health records would have to borrow. It judges
neither, and changes neither.

## The question each one asks

**Homestead asks what may cross a boundary.** Every field of every
record is given a rung by a five-step procedure, and every place a value
could appear has a ceiling. A field with no rung fails the build.

**Willow asks who approved this, and can it be proven.** A box answers
from a person's own files; code may search, suggest and record; only the
person's key seals, and a seal counts only while its proof holds.

One guards disclosure and the other guards decision. That is why they
fit together more than they overlap.

## Side by side

| | homestead | willow |
|---|---|---|
| the unit | a household, one operator | a box, one person's key |
| what is classified | every field: `L1` public in its forum, `L2` household, `L3` names a person, `L4` protected category, `L5` never served | nothing by sensitivity. Files have kinds (made, kept, test, and so on), and add-ons find secrets and personal data by their shape |
| where a value may appear | five surfaces, each with a ceiling: the list pane `L3`, the detail pane `L4`, a model's prompt `L2`, an agent's retrieval `L2`, an export `L2` or `L4` with a stated purpose | wherever the box is asked. A passage served to a person is the passage served to a model or an agent |
| the log | two: one the operator sees, holding references and never content; one hash-chained with its head kept apart, optionally keyed, optionally encrypted | one record, each row holding the hash of the one before, keeping acts and not words |
| the key | one file on the machine, optional, never escrowed; it makes the chain unforgeable | the person's, kept outside the box, readable only by them, never read by code; without it nothing is sealed |
| "sealed" means | encrypted, or at `L5` never shown | approved by the person |
| who checks the builder | "verified_by ≠ author": a second seat ratifies what the first proposed | the actor is never its own witness: code never approves, and what it suggests waits on the person |
| saying no | quietly. A refusal is information at the rung of the thing refused, so an absence is not rendered | aloud. Nothing found, an answer waiting and a seal that doesn't prove all end "waits on you" |
| advice | walled off. Public reference and a person's record never meet on one surface, so it cannot become a symptom checker | not invented. A passage is served only when it holds half the question's words. Nothing stops an add-on putting two piles side by side |
| leaving the machine | an export with a purpose, logged as an act with references and no content | the card's own line says whether an add-on reaches the network; sharing is an add-on's act on the record |

## Where they agree

- **Acts, not content, in the log.** Homestead learned it from a note
  that leaked into a prompt through an activity log. Willow's record
  keeps what was done and its hash, never what was said.
- **A name is a claim.** Homestead renamed a log that was called sealed
  when nothing about it was. Willow's add-on cards are held to what they
  promise by test.
- **The one who builds is not the one who approves.**
- **Nothing is invented.** One refuses to advise; the other refuses to
  answer past what the files hold.
- **No server, no account, no escrow.**

## What a health willow would have to borrow

1. **A ceiling by who is asking.** Today a box serves an agent the same
   passage it serves its owner. Homestead holds a model's prompt and an
   agent's retrieval to `L2`: nothing that names a person. For a pile of
   health records that is the first thing missing.
2. **A rung on what is served.** Willow classifies files by kind, not
   by what disclosing them would cost. Homestead's five steps are a
   procedure a person can follow and a build can check.
3. **A quiet no.** Willow's "waits on you" is right for an approval and
   wrong for a protected category, where saying that something is
   withheld gives it away.
4. **A purpose on anything that leaves.** The emergency card and the
   school form both leave with a declared purpose and a logged act.

## What homestead might take from willow

1. **The person's key as the approval.** Its key guards the log's
   integrity; nothing in it is a person's assent to a specific act that
   can be proven later.
2. **A unit smaller than the household.** Its own plan says the
   one-operator premise does not survive health unmodified. A box a
   person is that modification.
3. **Serving only what the pile holds, with the line it came from**, for
   the lane that reads public reference.

## What this reading cannot vouch for

It was made from documents and code at one commit each, by questions
and by search, in one sitting. The table's homestead column quotes its
rungs procedure and health plan; where that procedure calls itself
reconstructed, so is this. The willow column is from the kernel and the
constitution's table of clauses. Neither project's author has read it.
